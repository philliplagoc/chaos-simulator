# Devlog

One dated entry per session: what we built, what broke, one interesting decision.
This becomes the Substack draft.

## 2026-10-16
- **Built:** Godot 4.7.2 project with the Compatibility renderer, `Sim` and `EventBus` autoloads, a headless test runner.
- **Broke:** nothing yet.
- **Interesting decision:** every random number in the game comes from one seeded generator in `Sim`, so a seed replays the same starting city.

## 2026-10-17
- **Built:** finished Phase 0. Godot is on PATH, the Web export templates are installed, the code is on GitHub (https://github.com/philliplagoc/chaos-simulator), and the Kenney CC0 art packs are stored in `art/kenney/`.
- **Broke:** `godot` isn't found from Claude Code's `!` prompt, because that runs Git Bash, which can't find `godot.cmd` by name. Running Godot from PowerShell works. The first editor session ended without the templates installed, so we opened the editor a second time.
- **Interesting decision:** the raw art packs sit behind a `.gdignore` file, so Godot doesn't import 2,000+ images we won't use. Only the dozen sprites picked in Phase 10 get copied into `art/sprites/`.

## 2026-10-17 (Phase 1)
- **Built:** a throwaway performance spike: dots finding paths on a street grid with `AStarGrid2D`, exported as a single-threaded web build and uploaded as a draft on itch.io.
- **Broke:** nothing. Chrome held 60 FPS at 60, 120 and 240 dots locally, and 60 FPS on itch. Firefox wasn't tested (not installed); it still needs a check before launch.
- **Interesting decision:** we tested the scariest unknown, browser frame rate, before building any of the game. With 4x headroom at 240 dots, the full 40 citizens + 20 cars stay in, and the 30 + 15 fallback isn't needed.

## 2026-10-17 (Phase 2)
- **Built:** the district. `CityMap` lays out a 96x54-cell grid with 4 intersections, crosswalks and 9 named blocks, each with a marked entrance and curb. `Nav` adds three `AStarGrid2D` path-finding grids (cars, walkers, jaywalkers), and every pair of destinations is reachable both ways. The World scene draws it all, with drag-to-pan, scroll-to-zoom and a "best on desktop" banner. Tests: 438 checks.
- **Broke:** the banner's "narrow window" check could never fire. With stretch aspect "expand", Godot keeps the drawing area at least 1280 px wide, so a 600 px window still reported 1280. The fix checks the real window size (`get_window().size`). The check only runs at startup, so testing it means launching with `--resolution 800x720`.
- **Interesting decision:** the map is pure data (`RefCounted`, no nodes), so 438 checks run headless in seconds without opening a window. The whole district is drawn once in `_draw()` and cached, so 5,184 cells cost nothing per frame.

## 2026-10-17 (Phase 3)
- **Built:** traffic. Every intersection has a light where each incoming lane gets its own green in turn, followed by a 2-second all-red so turning cars can clear the junction. Lights write their colors to a shared board in `Sim`, and cars read it there, so no car ever holds a link to a light. 20 cars follow road paths in the right-hand lane, slow down for corners, brake for anything ahead, stop at the line before the crosswalk, and crash (with a danger signal) above 75 px/s. Tests: 450 checks. Five minutes of watching: 0 crashes, no pile-ups.
- **Broke:** nothing in the code, but the lamps confused the human tester. Drawn on the road center line, each lamp looked like it belonged to both directions. We moved them to the corners, one per corner, each at its drivers' near right.
- **Interesting decision:** cars at the city limits currently U-turn, and the tester wanted them to drive off and be replaced. We agreed, but deferred it to after the Road Rager (new task T039b). `car.gd` gets edited 7 more times before then, and car owners (Phase 4) must stay in town. Waiting means one change instead of eight.

## 2026-10-17 (Phase 4)
- **Built:** a sim clock (06:00 start, 1 real second = 2 sim minutes, a full day in 12 real minutes) and 40 citizens. Each one is generated from the seed with a name, a job, a home, a shift, money, hunger and patience. Every 2 sim minutes, a small scoring brain picks the goal with the highest score (work, deliver, home, café, bank, shop, park). Car owners drive from the lot to the curb, and everyone else walks the sidewalks. Impatient walkers jaywalk. Anyone near a `danger` runs away and sets off a one-step `panic` among the people around them. F3 shows each citizen's goal; right-click drops a test danger. Tests: 546 checks. The human playtest saw a morning rush, a quiet midday, an evening rush, and a crowd scattering from a right-click.
- **Broke:** fleeing citizens ran straight through buildings. Flee is a straight line away from the danger, and nothing checked what was in the way. Now a fleeing step that would land on a building cell is skipped, so people pile up against the wall instead. A throwaway headless run counted 14,794 frames of someone standing on a building before the fix and 0 after.
- **Interesting decision:** the brain has no randomness and no memory. It's a pure function of (needs, clock), so the morning and evening rushes come from shift times alone: people leave 45 minutes before shifts that start between 06:30 and 09:00, and head out again when shifts end between 16:30 and 18:00. Nothing schedules a rush; it shows up on its own.

## 2026-10-17 (Phase 5)
- **Built:** 300 short, silly bios (50 per job), handed out from the seed with no repeats, plus a click-to-inspect card: name, job, trait, goal, four bars (money, hunger, patience, courage) and the bio. While the card is open, the camera follows the citizen, a yellow ring marks them, and dragging the map stops the follow. Tests: 1,877 checks (900 of them make sure every bio follows the rules: length, uniqueness, banned words).
- **Broke:** nothing in the code, but the design. The tester's first question was "do these bios do anything?" They didn't: the spec only asked for flavor text. So each bio now carries one of six traits (hothead, hero, nervous, snacker, penny pincher, chill) that turns existing knobs: how fast hunger rises, how fast patience refills, when you go to the bank. Each trait also sets a courage value from flight to fight.
- **Interesting decision:** the trait is chosen from the bio, not the bio from the trait. I read all 300 and tagged each one by hand (the fish-microwaving office worker is a hothead, the twist-tie saver is a penny pincher), so the card's joke and the citizen's behavior agree. Courage won't do anything until the Car Thief exists. The plan now has a task (T039c) where brave citizens run *at* thefts instead of away and can tackle a thief on foot. They can also get flattened by the getaway car.

## 2026-10-17 (Phase 6)
- **Built:** things in the street that react. Hydrants burst into a blue puddle when hit hard; cars in the puddle brake at a quarter strength and fishtail across the lane until it dries up 60 sim minutes later. Fuel barrels and gas pumps light a 0.6-second fuse when hit, then explode with a big `explosion` danger that crashes cars close by and scatters walkers. Traffic lights have a pole; knock it (or blast it) and the intersection flashes yellow, and cars crawl through at 40. Pressing F fires a throwaway test car at the nearest object. Tests: 1,893 checks. The human check passed: puddle, double explosion and flashing light all worked first try.
- **Broke:** nothing at runtime, but one plan step no longer fit. The plan drew a dark dot for each light pole on the north-east corner, and since Phase 3 that corner already holds a lamp, which would have covered the dot. We dropped the dot: the lamp marks the pole.
- **Interesting decision:** no barrel knows about any other barrel. A barrel explodes by shouting `explosion` with a radius on the event bus, and every barrel listens for explosions that reach it. The double explosion at the gas station isn't written anywhere in the code; two barrels 2 cells apart simply hear each other. The same shout knocks out lights and crashes cars, for free.

## 2026-10-17 (Phase 7)
- **Built:** physics for hard hits. A car faster than 40 px/s, or a blast close by, sends a citizen tumbling as a little spinning starfish. Then they lie flat with yellow stars for 3 seconds, fade out, and walk out of their front door 30 seconds later. Cars hit at 130+ spin out, bounce off buildings and drive back to their lane. A hit at 220+ leaves a wreck that waits 40 sim minutes and then drives on. A car near the middle of a blast becomes a charred husk and is gone for good, and if its owner was driving, they're thrown out seeing stars. Only 5 bodies can fly at once. Anything past that just falls over where it is, which keeps the web build smooth. Tests: 1,893 checks. The human check passed after three rounds of feedback.
- **Broke:** the plan's version teleported. Buildings were only paint, so spinning cars slid straight through them. When they settled, they snapped back to the nearest road, sometimes from the middle of a block. Crashed cars got "towed", meaning they reappeared somewhere random. Parked cars hopped into their slots. And the F debug car vanished the instant it spun: its 3-second launch timer counted a spinout as "launch over, delete me". Now buildings are solid for flying bodies, and every car gets back to the road by driving there. A throwaway run watched all 20 cars for 3 sim hours and saw no move over 15 px in a frame.
- **Interesting decision:** a car's "back to normal" is one function, `_recover()`, that only looks at where the car ended up, not at what happened to it. It asks four things: burnt (remove it), parked (stay parked, or drive home if it landed on the road), still on its lane (keep going), or off the road (drive back first). Spinouts, fender-benders, wrecks, blasts and puddles all end there, so a new kind of hit never needs its own clean-up code.

## 2026-10-22 (Phase 8)
- **Built:** the two criminals and a menu to drop them. The Car Thief is a masked figure who walks straight through traffic to the nearest stopped car, pulls the driver out and races for a map exit, running every light. The Road Rager is a red car with an anger bar: stuck in traffic it tailgates at 30, swerves into the oncoming lane at 60 and rams the car ahead at 85. Citizens with courage 70+ now run *at* a theft instead of away (orange ring) and can tackle the thief on foot, or get flattened crossing the road to do it. Through-traffic finally leaves town: ambient cars drive off one edge and come back in at another, and burnt or stolen cars are replaced so the district keeps 20. Tests: 1,904 checks. The human check passed: a chain reaction in 3 of 5 tries for each criminal.
- **Broke:** the Rager kept leaving. The through-traffic change (T039b) sent every car without an owner to a map exit, and the Rager has no owner, so it drove off the map within 5 to 32 seconds in 4 of 5 test runs, before its anger could build. Now it wanders and never leaves. The tester also noticed a stolen car would sit politely in a queue at the lights. Now a stolen car overtakes through the oncoming lane as soon as it's blocked, and both getaway cars hop up onto the sidewalk when the oncoming lane is busy.
- **Interesting decision:** the stolen car and the Rager share one set of moves. The getaway is just road rage with the patience set to zero: the same overtake, the same sidewalk hop, the same ram. So every tweak to one criminal's driving shows up in the other for free. In a 3-minute test run, 9 of 16 overtakes went up on the sidewalk, and none of that was written as "thief behavior".

## 2026-10-22 (Phase 9)
- **Built:** the controls and the scoreboard. A top bar shows a chaos meter from 0 to 100 with a label (Calm, Rowdy, Chaotic, MAYHEM), the sim clock, Pause / 1x / 3x, Reset, New Seed and the current seed. Crashes add 12, explosions 20, thefts 10, tumbles 8, burst hydrants 5 and each panicked shout 1. Every fleeing citizen pushes it up 0.6 a second, and it drains 3 a second. Reset rebuilds the exact same city from the same seed, and New Seed builds a different one. A new test builds the real world three times (seed 42, 42, 43) and checks that the two 42s match and the 43 doesn't. Tests: 1,917 checks. The human check passed first try.
- **Broke:** nothing at runtime. Two plan steps said to replace the whole test runner with a fixed copy, but that copy was written before the car tests existed (Phase 8), so pasting it would have quietly dropped 5 checks. Each step got one new suite line instead.
- **Interesting decision:** the meter doesn't know about criminals, barrels or cars. It listens to the same `danger` shout every citizen hears, and it counts the people currently in the "fleeing" group. Anything we add later that makes noise or scares people moves the meter with no new code. The same-seed test also passed on its first run, which means eight phases of code never used randomness outside the one seeded generator.

## 2026-10-22 (Phase 10)
- **Built:** real art and a release candidate. Cars are now Kenney top-down race cars and people are top-down figures, one look per job. The thief is a suited figure, the Rager is the red car, and tumbling people and spinning cars keep their sprites mid-flight. F2 shows frames per second, the agent count and how many bodies are flying. A README and the itch page copy are written, the Phase 2 performance spike is deleted, and two sweeps found no look-alike game names and no keys or secrets. The full web build (10.5 MB zip) is on the itch draft. Tests: 1,917 checks. The human check passed: around 50 FPS in both Chrome and Firefox, so no need for the 30-citizen fallback.
- **Broke:** nothing. Every plan edit applied first time, and tests and smoke were clean on the first run.
- **Interesting decision:** the art is optional. Every sprite goes through one small `Art` helper that returns nothing if the file is missing, and each agent keeps its old drawing for that case: colored dots for people, boxes for cars. Overlays like brake lights, the thief's mask and the anger bar draw on top of the sprite, so none of the game logic had to change. A burnt car deliberately drops its sprite and spins as a black husk.
